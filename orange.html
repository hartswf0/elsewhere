The Orange Tree

The film, in words

  1. An orchard. A heavy orange tree hung with every project, 311 in all, colored by ripeness; the fallen ones lie in the grass. Everything I have made is still on the tree.
  2. A figure with square glasses and an orange scarf walks in, looks up, crouches, jumps, and picks one orange. The branch springs back, leaves fall, and a rotten orange drops. Pick one.
  3. A thumb presses into the orange and the peel comes away in one strip. Near the fruit it is peel; farther out it becomes film stock, one reel for each body of work. Peel it, and the skin comes off as film.
  4. The peeled orange is squeezed and bursts into nine segments, one per body of work, each named. Inside, it was always in segments.
  5. On an animator's light table the orange is dropped as a bouncing ball, drawing by drawing, until it rolls to rest. The first thing an animator learns to drop is a ball. Mine is an orange.
  6. The figure walks onto the drawing, picks the orange up, and offers it to you. Pick one. Walk into the grove.
